# /bb:review

## Goal
The client only ever reviews taste. Facts are our job, so a client never finds a mistake we could have caught, and every note they give is done, shown and closed in one round.

## Why
In Nov 2026 a client got a misspelt discount code on statics and paid ads, an email calling a product discount "site-wide", and first cuts on the day the work was due. Her words: "you or someone in your team need to oversee emails/statics or everything before it comes to me for review." Each slip was small. Together they cost trust and a day of rework, and they put our ad spend behind a code that would not work.

## Who signs off
- A senior checks before anything is sent: **Tian or Roy**. It is never the person who made the piece.
- Anyone can run this skill to prepare the check. Only a senior's yes sends. Claude never sends anything to a client.

## 1. Plan the timeline when the work is briefed
Work back from the live date:
- The first version goes to the client **5 working days before going live**.
- The client gets **2 to 3 days** to give feedback, and we get **1 to 2 days** for revisions.
- Never produce and revise on the same day.

Tell the client these dates when the work is briefed. If the timeline gets squeezed, tell the senior the day it happens, not on the deadline.

Shoots: the concept and script are signed off by the client in writing before the shoot. Once signed, the concept is fixed and a new concept is new work. Edits (the cut, music, colour, pacing, captions) are always open to change.

## 2. Check before it goes
Go through every piece one at a time: each size of each video and static, each email, each page.

1. **Offer facts against the store, read today.** Don't check against the brief, the deck or last year. Check:
   - the code, spelt exactly as in the store's discount
   - the percentage
   - which products it covers (never "site-wide" or "everything" unless the store discount says so)
   - start and end dates and times
   - minimum spend and free-shipping line
   - stock or limit claims ("only 50")

   If the discount isn't built in the store yet, the piece waits or the claim comes out.
2. **Same facts everywhere.** The ad copy, statics, video end card, email, site banner and Meta's stored promo code all say the same code and the same claim.
3. **Words.** Check spelling, names, prices and the client's own terms for their products.
4. **Brand.** The brand bible's colours, fonts and logo, plus the client's video rules. A colour that drifted from the bible is a fail.
5. **Brief.** It matches the signed-off script beat for beat. Anything we changed is called out, not hidden.
6. **Technical.** Check:
   - the right sizes and safe zones
   - sound levels
   - links that work and land on the right page
   - tracking tags
   - the AI enhancements on ads, which must be off

**Output: a one-page check sheet.** Each piece gets pass or fix, with proof: a frame, a screenshot, or the store reading with its time. Fix everything, re-check the fixed pieces, then hand the sheet and the work to the senior for the yes. Say plainly what you could not check.

## 3. When feedback comes in
1. **Capture it word for word.** Take it from WhatsApp, email, the call or comments and turn it into a numbered list in the job's notes. One note per line, quoting the client, with who said it and when. Merge repeats, but drop nothing.
2. **Sort each line:**
   - **edit:** we do it
   - **fact:** check it against the store
   - **unclear:** a question for the call
   - **new concept:** outside what was signed, so the senior decides
   - **clashes with our rules:** for example dates on ads, so the senior decides
3. **Call the client the same day** to walk the list. Write the agreed outcome on each line.
4. **Give each line an owner** and a due date inside the 1 to 2 days.
5. **Done means every line is ticked with proof**, a before and after frame or a screenshot. Run step 2 again on the changed pieces, get the senior's yes, then send it back with the list: each of their notes and what we did.
6. **A mistake made twice becomes a rule** in the client's notes or the job's skill, so it can't happen a third time.

## 4. When the client is upset
- **The angry boat:** only one person fits in it. If we climb in, it sinks. Don't defend. Agree with what's true, own our mistakes in one plain sentence, then steer to the list and the fix. We're on the same side.
- **Kind and firm.** Detailed feedback is welcome. Rudeness to the team isn't, and a senior steps in when that happens.
- **On the call, keep "we'll fix that" (edits) apart from "that's a new idea" (concept)** without arguing. The senior handles scope afterwards.
- Look for the frustration underneath. It's usually time or trust, and the fix is the process above, shown to them.

## Hard limits
- Nothing reaches a client without a senior's yes. Claude never sends.
- Never mark a fact as checked unless you read it from the source today. Name what you didn't check.
- Never put one client's name, work or numbers in another client's check.
