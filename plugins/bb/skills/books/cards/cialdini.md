# Influence
**Robert Cialdini.** Read in full, 518 passages.

## The core idea
Rushed, distracted or unsure people decide on one reliable cue, and seven cues reliably get a yes. Later, the book moves from "like us" to "one of us", arguing that shared identity moves people more than shared taste. It ends by saying overload makes these shortcuts more common, so using a true cue is fair and faking one is the betrayal.

## What this changes
- A generic hook says everyone has the problem. We show one person with it and save the crowd for the solution.
- A generic script hides the flaw and stacks reasons. We admit one flaw early, go straight to the best benefit, and use one or two levers.
- Generic proof says "thousands of happy customers". We use peers who match the viewer, or real growth if the brand is young, and we let proof arrive in passing.
- Generic urgency says "selling fast" whatever the stock. We use a true count, or if stock is endless, the thing only this brand has.
- A generic edit puts music under everything and the logo at the end. We choose feeling or facts for each ad, and keep the product in frame on the pleasant beats.

## The ten best passages
1. `cialdini-0026` · Ch 1 · The worse option shown first makes the real one shine, and cheap shown first makes dear look dearer. · Open a comparison on the poor or costly alternative, then cut to the product. Reveal prices big to small.
2. `cialdini-0047` · Ch 2 · Two gifts of equal value got different results: a key ring lifted food spend 12 percent, yoghurt 24 percent. · Pick the free extra that answers what the viewer wants right now, and name it before the ask.
3. `cialdini-0476` · Ch 3 · Putting a brand over pleasant pictures five or six times raised liking, and viewers didn't notice. · Keep the product or logo in frame on every pleasant beat instead of saving it for the end card.
4. `cialdini-0194` · Ch 4 · A sign showing three thieves nearly tripled theft, while one showing a lone thief cut it below the baseline. · Show the problem on one person and put the crowd in the solution shot.
5. `cialdini-0196` · Ch 4 · A small number hurts on its own, but a small number that is rising makes people join. Cialdini reversed his own earlier advice on this. · For a young brand, put a real before and after figure early, and never say "only a few".
6. `cialdini-0240` · Ch 5 · The moment after admitting a flaw is when a claim lands hardest. · Write the flaw, then the single best benefit on the very next line, with no filler or cutaway between them.
7. `cialdini-0259` · Ch 6 · Scarcity can be about what a product is, not just how many are left. · If stock is endless, drop the low-stock line. Name what can't be had elsewhere and what the viewer misses without it.
8. `cialdini-0330` · Ch 7 · Naming shoppers by a flattering identity took purchases from 32 to 51 percent, beating a price message at 46. · Test an opening that names who the viewer is against a price-led opening.
9. `cialdini-0405` · Ch 8 · One line of true shared identity did what scholarship couldn't, and it left the rival outside the group. · Find the one identity the speaker really shares with the viewer and say it in the first line, before any product talk.
10. `cialdini-0413` · Ch 8 · Music suits people buying for pleasure and facts suit practical buyers, so the treatment must match the mindset. · Decide per ad before cutting. Score pleasure products with music, and keep considered purchases clear so the claims are heard.

## Three questions for the critic
1. If the draft shows the problem, is it on one person, with the crowd kept for the solution? (`cialdini-0194`)
2. If the draft admits a flaw, does it come early, with the single best benefit straight after it? (`cialdini-0240`)
3. If stock isn't really short, does the offer sell something unavailable elsewhere and carry no low-stock line? (`cialdini-0259`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Asking big, then retreating:** this relies on hearing a no first (`cialdini-0060`). A video can only show the big option and drop straight to the smaller one (`cialdini-0471`).
- **Spoken and written commitments:** these rely on a real answer, and a viewer gives none (`cialdini-0315`, `cialdini-0339`). A comment prompt is a weaker cousin, so don't expect the book's lifts.
- **The because result:** this comes from a small favour at a copier (`cialdini-0012`). It is not evidence for a purchase.
- **The numbers:** most come from letters, signs, shops, websites and lab tasks, not short video. Use them to choose an approach and never quote them as forecasts. The picture pairing and luncheon work is especially far from a phone feed (`cialdini-0121`, `cialdini-0476`).
- **The pairing counts:** five or six repeats came from separate studies using still pictures. Take the principle, not the count, into a 15 second cut.
- **Sales floor tricks:** low-balling, planted crowds, fake can't-come-back lines and actors posing as customers are out (`cialdini-0162`, `cialdini-0453`). Inside news and banned angles only work if true (`cialdini-0273`, `cialdini-0492`).
- **The rest:** the Milgram, cult, copycat suicide and Jonestown material explains the theory but gives nothing to script. The defence sections are written for the buyer, not the maker.
