# Scientific Advertising
**Claude Hopkins.** Read in full, 78 passages.

## The core idea
Advertising is one salesperson talking to one buyer, multiplied, and only traced cost per customer can judge it. The early chapters set the rules for a single ad: hail the right buyer, offer service, be specific, tell the whole story. The later chapters move around the ad: research what people do now, find what rivals haven't said, test small, keep what beats the current best, and show the bright side.

## What this changes
- A generic script opens wide to win views. We call out only people who could buy, and accept losing the rest.
- A generic script says "premium quality". We use an exact figure or an ordinary process fact no rival has told.
- A generic script opens on the pain. We show the result the buyer wants, and a problem opening must beat that in a test.
- A generic script fights the rival brand. We first find what people do now instead of buying, which may be no brand at all.
- A generic test crowns one winner. We run the same body under many hooks and keep weaker appeals that still pay.

## The ten best passages
- `hopkins-0009` · Ch 2 · Sell to one half-sold person, using what real customers say. · Name one buyer, write as if across a counter, and take lines from reviews and DMs.
- `hopkins-0010` · Ch 3 · People buy when they are served, not asked. · Open and close on what the viewer gets, and drop "buy our brand" language.
- `hopkins-0018` · Ch 5 · The opening selects buyers, it doesn't gather a crowd. · Make the first line or frame name the buyer or their situation, and cut clever openings that draw non-buyers.
- `hopkins-0020` · Ch 5 · Hook tests show how much to run each appeal, not just which one wins. · Log every hook by appeal, and keep a weaker appeal running if it still pays.
- `hopkins-0027` · Ch 7 · An exact figure shows that you tested. · Replace each vague claim such as best or amazing with a true measured number, on screen where it helps.
- `hopkins-0029` · Ch 7 · The first brand to explain the ordinary process can win with it. · Film the normal making steps with real counts, even if every rival does the same.
- `hopkins-0031` · Ch 8 · Every viewer is a first meeting, and possibly the last. · Assume they have never heard of the brand, and cut anything aimed at current customers.
- `hopkins-0043` · Ch 11 · The real competitor may be the buyer's current habit. · Find out what most prospects do now, then make the video about switching from that.
- `hopkins-0047` · Ch 12 · Work out how to win one satisfied rival user before speaking to thousands. · Write the script as your answer to that person, built on one true advantage rivals haven't stated.
- `hopkins-0068` · Ch 18 · The buyer already knows the problem, so show where they want to be. · Show the after state and the envied person, and say what to do, not what to avoid.

## Three questions for the critic
1. Does the opening call out only people who could buy, even if a wider or cleverer one would pull more views? (`hopkins-0018`)
2. Does the draft carry at least one exact figure or process fact, with no superlative standing in for it? (`hopkins-0029`)
3. Does the video show the result the buyer wants, and tell them what to do rather than what to avoid? (`hopkins-0068`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Only interested people read:** the feed puts ads in front of people who didn't choose them, so the first seconds must also stop the scroll. Still hail the buyer.
- **"Don't try to be amusing":** too strict for social video. Keep funny work if the laugh carries the product point (`hopkins-0008`).
- **Tell the full story:** 60 seconds can't hold it. Keep the strongest claims, in order, and let the page carry the rest.
- **Before and after:** "follies of the past" and "four to one" come from his own print results. Test them on phone video rather than treating them as law.
- **Never changing an ad:** his ads ran for years, but a short video can tire faster on a feed. Keep the proven appeal and refresh the execution.
- **Print and post:** type size, colour costs, coupons, dealers and paid inserts are period mechanics. Keep the tests underneath: does this shot sell, and what does a customer cost?
- **Free samples:** only use them when the client really offers one. Otherwise, lower the risk with what is real, such as returns.
- **Trust in claims:** he assumes readers don't expect ads to lie. Don't count on that, and use only figures that are real and checked.
