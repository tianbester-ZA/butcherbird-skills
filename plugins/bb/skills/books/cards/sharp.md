# How Brands Grow
**Byron Sharp.** Read in full, 235 passages.

## The core idea
Brands grow mainly by winning more buyers, most of whom buy rarely and barely think about the brand. Ads therefore work less by persuading and more by being noticed, linked to the right brand and refreshing memory. Late in the book this becomes a creative brief: use distinctive assets, tell the same story in fresh ways, and teach new brands the basics first.

## What this changes
- Generic scripts speak to a narrow persona. We write for any category buyer, including someone who has never bought.
- Generic scripts hunt for a unique claim. We use one true fact, and only if it leaves room for the brand.
- Generic edits save the brand for the end card. We build the brand into the idea.
- Generic batches get a new look per concept. We keep the cues fixed and change the situation and hook.
- Generic thinking asks whether the ad convinces. We ask whether people will notice it, know whose it is and remember it.

## The ten best passages

`sharp-0011` · Ch 1
- **Lesson:** An ad has two hurdles, and most fail: being noticed and being linked to the right brand.
- **Do:** Test every cut on both. A gripping open with an unclear brand still fails.

`sharp-0026` · Ch 2
- **Lesson:** Advertising can't easily teach a new habit or use.
- **Do:** Show the product in the role people already expect. If the new use is the whole script, rethink it.

`sharp-0119` · Ch 8
- **Lesson:** New campaigns usually focus on what's fresh, when the branding should carry over.
- **Do:** Lock the colour, type, logo position and sound cue before writing a new concept.

`sharp-0126` · Ch 9
- **Lesson:** A tiny nudge among occasional buyers can matter, with no new argument at all.
- **Do:** Let some reels simply remind people of the brand, its pleasure and its moment. Don't add reasons out of nerves.

`sharp-0130` · Ch 9
- **Lesson:** The strongest sales ads in this evidence were likeable and visual, not hard-selling.
- **Do:** Make the picture carry the idea. Cut voiceover claims that only repeat what's on screen.

`sharp-0139` · Ch 9
- **Lesson:** The brand name and the product in use are core, while a persuasive fact is optional.
- **Do:** Say or show the name, and show the product being used. Add a fact only if it crowds out neither.

`sharp-0193` · Ch 11
- **Lesson:** A popular idea can build memory of itself and not the brand, and Miller Lite kept losing sales.
- **Do:** Retell the concept in one line. If the brand can drop out, rebuild the idea around it before filming.

`sharp-0194` · Ch 11
- **Lesson:** News should sit inside the brand's existing story, not replace it.
- **Do:** Put launches and new variants into the client's familiar format, and don't invent a format for each.

`sharp-0195` · Ch 11
- **Lesson:** A strong asset can brand an ad alone, and people recognise it without thinking about its meaning.
- **Do:** Find the one object, colour or sound that says the brand, and get it into raw or creator footage. Don't spend script time explaining it.

`sharp-0196` · Ch 11
- **Lesson:** Consistency doesn't mean rerunning the same ad, because the art is fresh versions of one story.
- **Do:** Fix one story shape per brand. Vary the situation, casting and hook.

## Three questions for the critic
1. In the opening, is there something a stranger would notice and link to this brand? (`sharp-0011`)
2. If a viewer retold this reel to a friend, would the brand have to be in the retelling? (`sharp-0193`)
3. Set beside this brand's last reel, is it clearly the same brand, with new situations and hooks? (`sharp-0119`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **The 84% figure:** It comes from 143 Australian TV ads and phone recall. It isn't a benchmark for social video.
- **Timings:** The book gives no rules for seconds or opening frames. "First second" advice is our translation.
- **Rhythm:** It says nothing useful about pace, captions, music or cuts.
- **New brands:** "Just refresh" assumes memories already exist. Small or new clients must first teach the name, what it does, how it looks and where to buy it (`sharp-0193`).
- **Direct response:** The memory argument explicitly sets this aside (`sharp-0133`). It's no reason to bury the offer in a reel built to sell during a live sale.
- **Priming:** Sharp doubts the real-world strength of the Halloween and dog studies himself (`sharp-0139`). Don't build a script on them.
- **Media planning:** Reach and continuity are buying decisions. A script or cut can only supply cues that repeat across reels.
- **Creative ideas:** The book says to be consistent and entertaining, not how to find a good idea.
