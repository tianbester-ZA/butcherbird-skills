# Breakthrough Advertising
**Eugene Schwartz.** Read in full, 236 passages.

## The core idea
Copy cannot create desire. It can only channel a want that already exists, and where it opens depends on what the market knows and what claims it has already heard. Late in the book the argument moves from what to say to the order you say it in: belief comes from preparation, so an ad is an architecture of reactions, not a list of claims.

## What this changes
- A generic script opens on its biggest claim. We test a smaller claim the viewer accepts at once, then bridge to the big one.
- A generic script stacks benefits. We cut to one promise and spend the saved seconds making it believed.
- A generic voiceover says "premium" or "confident". We show status through casting, setting and props, and keep spoken lines for performance.
- A generic script hides every weakness. We admit a real flaw straight before the benefit.
- A generic edit cuts to a template or a timer. We cut where the viewer would start wanting proof, a mechanism or the next promise.

## The ten best passages
Pages are blank in our copy, so these are listed by section.

- `schwartz-0049` · The List Never Ends · A cold hook must turn away as many people as it attracts. · Ask if the target viewer would see the hook as their own problem and state of mind, and let the wrong viewers leave.
- `schwartz-0076` · On the Only Type of Prevention Headline That Will Sell · People won't picture future harm happening to themselves. · In prevention ads, show the problem hitting a child, partner or other loved one.
- `schwartz-0114` · Applying Intensification to the Campaign · A startling opener lends its jolt to a fixed message. · Lock the body and close, then test many openers on the same cut.
- `schwartz-0136` · How to Build New Images Into Your Product · Glamour sounds corny when said and goes unnoticed when shown. · Carry status and mood through the car, setting and tools, with no voiceover line about them.
- `schwartz-0148` · A New Definition of Awareness · The strongest claim is often not the strongest opener. · Split the big-claim hook against a softer, believable one that bridges to the same claim.
- `schwartz-0150` · A Detailed Example · Agreement comes before the first promise. · Open on a suspicion the viewer already holds, add a question nearly all would say yes to, then promise.
- `schwartz-0187` · Stage Three: Feature the Mechanism · A deal with no reason behind it isn't believed. · Give the true cause (such as excess stock) before saying the price.
- `schwartz-0207` · Believability-Borrowing Strategy #3 · Knocking your own offer deepens belief in the benefits. · Add one honest downside early, such as who it isn't for, then bring in the benefits.
- `schwartz-0213` · Verification · Proof is dull until the viewer is hungry for it. · Make the problem urgent first, then reveal the stat, test or review as the answer.
- `schwartz-0234` · Mood · Rhythm and word choice decide whether a piece feels forceful or sincere. · Pick one per reel: verb-led claims cut hard on the beat, or plain speech with the adjectives stripped and a self-deprecating line.

## Three questions for the critic
1. Would the target viewer accept the first line as true at first glance, before any proof? (`schwartz-0148`)
2. Is every status or identity idea carried by what's in frame, with no line saying it out loud? (`schwartz-0136`)
3. Does each proof arrive straight after the moment that makes the viewer want it? (`schwartz-0213`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Length:** the long-copy case, the promise catalogues and the multi-paragraph price story don't fit. In a short video, keep to one yes, one proof and one promise.
- **Long sentences:** the clumsy, unscannable sentences in `schwartz-0235` belong to print. Only the plain, unpolished feel transfers.
- **Camouflage:** `schwartz-0205` says to disguise the ad as news. Borrow the plain tone and handheld feel, but never fake a report, an expert or a reaction, and keep paid content labelled.
- **Escalating claims:** the "wonder drug" and "floats fat" headlines and the bigger-claim race won't pass today's ad rules or platform review. Any mechanism must be real.
- **Period examples:** the virility, cigarette and housewife images are dated, as are the prices and the American mass-market context. Keep the logic and replace the pictures.
- **Print craft:** matching a publication's type, datelines and by-lines has no direct use. Only the principle of looking native to the feed survives.
