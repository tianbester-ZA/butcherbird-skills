# $100M Leads (free course)
**Alex Hormozi.** Read in full, 342 passages.

## The core idea
Most businesses lack leads because they don't advertise enough, so the answer is more volume and more tests, with a free offer people actually want. Every piece of content has to hook, retain and reward. Late on, the argument moves from what you do to who advertises for you: customers, staff, agencies and affiliates. That only works if the product truly delivers, so expectations should rest on real average results.

## What this changes
- A generic script writes one hook. We write four types and treat the opening as the main test.
- A generic edit trims to a duration. We cut any second that gives nothing new, whatever the length.
- A generic ad says who it's for. We show it through casting, setting and the problem on screen.
- A generic payoff has the buyer state the benefit. We show someone else reacting.
- A generic proof beat uses the best testimonial. We prefer what customers typically got, stated as fact.

## Three questions for the critic
1. With the sound off, can the buyer recognise themselves from the opening picture alone? (`hormozi-leads-0180`)
2. Does the video deliver everything the hook promised, with nothing the viewer already knew? (`hormozi-leads-0101`)
3. Does the result happen on screen in real time, with no cutaway before it lands? (`hormozi-leads-0073`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Five seconds:** this is a third of a 15 second video, so the opening has to work faster. The advice to blow their minds in under 30 seconds comes from cold outreach and can't be used as a timing rule here.
- **Thin evidence:** the 200x jump is one story about views, not sales. The reading-level lift came from cold email, so simple words are the lesson, not a third grade level as a rule.
- **Numbers not to repeat:** the 80% on the opening, the claim that 90 percent of communication is nonverbal, and the 11 times more figure are rules of thumb. The last one changes between top 1% and top 0.1% within the book.
- **Volume:** 30 hooks on 10 bodies suits very large budgets. Keep the modular idea, at 3 to 5 hooks per body.
- **Ask ratios:** 10 to 1 applies to organic. A paid ad is always an ask, so it needs the reward inside the ad.
- **Limits:** the idea that any reason will do for a deadline only works here if the limit is true.
- **Callouts:** the bizarre headlines (government cheques, pizza weight loss) and body-based yes questions read as dated clickbait. Check them against platform ad rules before use.
- **Teasers:** the advice not to tease is for strangers. The whisper teaser (`0302`) is for launches to people who already follow the brand.
- **Not relevant to these videos:** warm and cold outreach, hiring, agency management, affiliate payouts and customer value maths. Lead magnets, webinars and booked calls also assume a higher-priced sale, so for ecommerce read them as a sample, a bundle or a first order deal.
