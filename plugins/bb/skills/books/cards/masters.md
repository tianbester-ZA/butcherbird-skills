# Masters of Copywriting
**Masters of Copywriting (various).** Read in full, 185 passages.

## The core idea
You can't create desire. You find a want people already have, and the selling power sits in the product, so preparation beats talent. Belief then comes from the order you say things in, not from bigger promises. Late on, the book adds story: the pitch must be the sensible ending of the story told. It closes on Schwartz's warning that every new ad is a fresh problem.

## What this changes
- Generic scripts open on the biggest benefit. We open on something the viewer accepts and hold the boldest claim until a proof beat has prepared it.
- Generic scripts stack benefits. We cut until one claim is fully believed.
- Generic fixes for a tired ad re-edit it or swap the format. We find something new the product does and build from that.
- Generic scripts chase a good line. If someone praises the line, it failed; they should be reacting to the product.
- Generic scripts start from the brief. We go through the product material and customer words first, and take the hook from the picture that emerges.

## The ten best passages

`masters-0021` · Second Action: Analyzing Your Product
- **Lesson:** One desire, one claim and one mechanism, aligned, make the theme.
- **Do:** Write that one-line brief first. Delete any shot or line that serves none of the three.

`masters-0060` · The Headline Does Not Sell
- **Lesson:** The same claim is believed in the middle and thrown away at the top.
- **Do:** Move the boldest claim to after the hook and a proof beat. Open on something easier to accept.

`masters-0077` · Think About Your "Does" Product
- **Lesson:** A format change adds only 20% to 30% pull; a new "does" reopens the same audience.
- **Do:** List the things the product does for the buyer. When an angle tires, make a new video from an unused one.

`masters-0097` · Assembling Words: Windows in a Store
- **Lesson:** Ordinary words in a fresh combination, including one word advertising avoids, beat pretty ones.
- **Do:** Cut any line or edit trick the viewer would notice as such. Keep the plain, slightly wrong word that names the real feeling.

`masters-0114` · Required Reading and Movies
- **Lesson:** Each unit should hit like a headline, and the desire behind it may be one people are ashamed of.
- **Do:** Check that each beat could stand alone as a hook. Say what the buyer secretly wants, not the respectable version.

`masters-0116` · The Engine that Moves Copy
- **Lesson:** When all claims blur, a strange mechanism makes people believe the claim again.
- **Do:** Pair a strong result with an odd how, and pay off the how later in the video.

`masters-0124` · What Your Prospect's State of Awareness Demands
- **Lesson:** For an unaware market, name what people are, and let it repel as many as it attracts.
- **Do:** Open cold videos on a shared attitude or situation, with no price, name or direct claim. A line that excludes nobody is wrong.

`masters-0126` · Intensification
- **Lesson:** People leave with one image, and showing it again from a new angle builds weight.
- **Do:** Choose the one image before the shoot. Vary how it is seen, never the message, and don't repeat a phrase.

`masters-0138` · Verification, Reinforcement, Interweaving
- **Lesson:** Keep raising a question and implying a later answer, while rhythm carries the mood unnoticed.
- **Do:** Plant one incomplete statement early and pay it off late. Set the mood through the pace of the cut before adding any effect.

`masters-0174` · A Final Word from Eugene Schwartz
- **Lesson:** Past winners give starting points only; the answer comes from asking the right questions about this problem.
- **Do:** Before borrowing a winning hook, answer who wants this, what they know and what they believe. Write the first line from those answers.

## Three questions for the critic
1. Is the boldest claim held back until something has prepared the viewer to believe it? (`masters-0060`)
2. Has the script cut down to one claim the viewer would fully believe? (`masters-0129`)
3. Would a viewer react to the product, not to the line or the edit? (`masters-0097`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Volume:** Envelopes with 30 to 50 claims and hundred-word headlines rely on a reader who can stop and browse. A short video carries one claim.
- **Timing:** The ten-second envelope and the three-minute film jolt are mail and cinema pacing. Keep the idea, not the numbers.
- **The long middle:** The thirteen ways to build desire are a menu. A short video has room for one or two, and a very short one may only fit a single accepted line before the claim.
- **Open loops:** "Go to page 71" has no equivalent. The answer must land inside the video.
- **Biographies:** Long expert credentials shrink to one concrete detail.
- **Claims:** Arthritis cures, "pain-proofed" and sexual congress show the method. They are not language to copy.
- **The rest:** Timer rituals, the Mozart story, the need lists (Maslow, Hill) and the guru material give a script or cut nothing to do.
