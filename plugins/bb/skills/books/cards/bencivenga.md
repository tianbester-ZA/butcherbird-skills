# Bencivenga Bullets
**Gary Bencivenga.** Read in full, 117 passages.

## The core idea
Every promise now meets "Yeah, sure", so a sale rests on belief: a claim no bigger than its proof, with a real reason behind it. The book then asks where the message comes from and answers late on: dig in the product for the one thing only it does, then show that benefit through the viewer's life. What you say matters more than how you say it, and the later bullets on offers, openings and metaphor serve that message.

## What this changes
- A generic script opens on a big promise and proves it later. Here, claim and proof arrive in the same line, and the claim shrinks to fit the proof.
- A generic script starts from a persona and the category's obvious benefit. Here, the viewer's want is the way in, but the message comes from what only this product does.
- A generic offer just says 50% off. Here, it carries a true reason, and buy one, get one free gets tested first.
- A generic edit keeps a clever line because it is fun. Here, it stays only if it carries the product's advantage.
- A generic writer drafts from the brief. Here, founder questions and far more facts than the video can hold come first.

## The ten best passages
- `bencivenga-0005` · Part 1 · Proof sets the size of the claim. · Cut each hook claim down to what the next few words can prove, then say both together.
- `bencivenga-0027` · Part 2 · A sale answers why you, why true and why now. · Give each question one short beat; if one is missing, the script isn't finished.
- `bencivenga-0029` · Part 2 · A discount without a reason doesn't register. · Say the true reason in the same line as the price, and never invent one.
- `bencivenga-0041` · Part 2 · The market is a want, not a demographic. · Open on the headache or desire, not on who the viewer is, and name the emotion each scene should carry.
- `bencivenga-0053` · Part 3 · People want one specific thing, not a range. · Give each video one named product and one use, and name it in the hook.
- `bencivenga-0067` · Part 3 · The product is the star, not the writer. · Ask the founder the ten questions before scripting, and cut any joke or pun that points away from the advantage.
- `bencivenga-0074` · Part 4 · Leading only with the obvious want gets screened out. · Drop the category benefit and build the hook on how this product is made, how long it lasts or what is in it.
- `bencivenga-0075` · Part 4 · The best execution is a demonstration of the unique benefit. · Find the one shot that proves it, like two closed fists opening, and build the cut around it.
- `bencivenga-0077` · Part 4, Bullet 21 · The same deal pulls differently depending on the wording. · Where margins allow, say buy one, get one free, not 50% off or half price.
- `bencivenga-0092` · Part 4 · The opening must matter to someone with no interest in listening. · Write 30 to 50 hooks, read each as if waking someone, and rewrite any they would sleep through.

## Three questions for the critic
- Does every claim sit in the same line as its proof, and is it no bigger than that proof? (`bencivenga-0005`)
- Would the core message become false if a rival's name replaced this product's? (`bencivenga-0074`)
- Does every discount or deadline carry a true reason, said in the same line? (`bencivenga-0029`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Formats:** long sales presentations, mini sites, direct mail, newspaper timing and matchbook ads are all dated. Keep the thinking, not the format.
- **Length:** a 15 second video can't argue at length. Why you, why true and why now may get only a few words each.
- **The ban on entertaining:** this is too absolute for a social feed, where a funny idea can earn the stop. Keep the test: does it land on the product's advantage?
- **Numbers:** the 40 percent, 71 percent and five-times figures come from other media and eras. Use them to choose what to test, never as forecasts.
- **Testimonials:** the book's view that they are overused is about written copy. It doesn't settle whether a real customer on camera works.
- **Late chapters:** the 80/20 productivity bullets, life advice and personal stories give a script nothing.
- **Edits:** there is almost nothing on feel, rhythm or the eye. Use this book to decide what a video says, and others to decide how it cuts.
