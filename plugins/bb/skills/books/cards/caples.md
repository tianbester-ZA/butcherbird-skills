# Tested Advertising Methods
**John Caples.** Read in full, 373 passages.

## The core idea
Finding the right appeal and putting it in the opening beats any polish, because the same space can sell 19.5 times more with the right one. Plain, specific facts then outsell fine writing. The book's late chapters shift from rules to method: opinion misleads, so test many openers against one control, and build everything on the winning appeal.

## What this changes
- **Appeal:** a generic script picks one sensible angle and polishes it. We hunt the appeal first and treat the writing as the small job.
- **Opening:** a generic script warms up, or teases with curiosity. We delete the warm-up and open on who it's for and what they get.
- **Claims:** a generic script stacks adjectives and tidy numbers. We use exact, checkable figures and never round them.
- **Picture:** a generic edit reaches for striking or symbolic shots. We use the literal product, in a frame that says what the first line says.
- **Length and choice:** a generic plan sets length by format and picks the cleverest cut. We set length by how much the viewer doubts, and sales pick the winner.

## The ten best passages
1. `caples-0009` · The New Advertising Strategy · The wrong appeal can't be rescued by craft. · Settle the one appeal before writing a line, and cut rough versions of different appeals before polishing any.
2. `caples-0077` · Finding the Right Appeal · All five winners had one appeal in the headline, while some losers had it only in the body. · Rank the client's past videos by sales, find what the winners share, and put it in the first line and on-screen text.
3. `caples-0113` · How to Write the First Paragraph · Some writers need a few lines to warm up before they say anything worthwhile. · Delete the first line or two of every script, then begin at the first useful line.
4. `caples-0156` · Twenty Ways to Increase Selling Power of Copy · A campaign built on one exact fact (the product beat the government standard by 52.7%) was so powerful the industry's association asked for it to be toned down. · Keep the brand's exact figure in voice and on screen, and name one specific feature instead of three adjectives.
5. `caples-0161` · Twenty Ways to Increase Selling Power of Copy · The smallest cut of an ad was the best copy. · Write the 60 first, cut to 30, then 15, then bring what survives back into the longer cuts.
6. `caples-0205` · How to Appeal to the Masses · People read the picture first, so a mismatch makes them misread the ad. · Make frame one literally show what line one says, and check for any second reading.
7. `caples-0248` · How to Make Small Ads Pay · Doubt sets the length, not the format. · Keep easy-to-explain products short, and give doubted products more time for the explanation and several customers.
8. `caples-0279` · Seventeen Ways to Test Your Advertising · Reviewers vote against embarrassing appeals and for clever ads, but sales can go the other way. · Don't drop an uncomfortable or plain version because the room dislikes it. Put it in the test.
9. `caples-0315` · Seventeen Ways to Test Your Advertising · Thirty-six headlines were each run against a single control ad, and two came out far ahead. · Write many openers for one offer, rank them by percentage over the control, and build on the top two.
10. `caples-0370` · Fifteen Famous Ads · Ogilvy's Rolls-Royce ad, the most talked-about of its time, used a very long headline and 719 words, all facts. · In a 30 to 60, let one checkable fact follow another, and let the hook run long if every word is a fact.

## Three questions for the critic
- Is the main appeal in the first spoken line and the on-screen text, not only later in the script? (`caples-0077`)
- Is every claim backed by an exact figure or named fact, with no rounded numbers or stacked adjectives? (`caples-0156`)
- Does the first frame show exactly what the first line says, so a skimmer can't take it for something else? (`caples-0205`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **Motion and sound:** the book has nothing on pace, music, cuts or performance. Its rhythm advice is about reading and speaking.
- **Long copy:** "the more you tell" comes from full pages. At 15 seconds, one appeal and one or two facts is the limit.
- **Test mechanics:** coupons, key numbers and split newspaper runs become tracked links and codes. A same-ad-set race isn't an even split, and our media rule puts new work in a new ad set.
- **Measures:** reply counts become purchases and cost per sale. The book's reply counts (under fifty per ad was too few, over 400 was plenty) are print figures, not thresholds for Meta.
- **Curiosity and gloom:** these rankings come from print. Treat "avoid curiosity" and "avoid the negative" as things to test, since some of the book's own winners use a problem or a gap.
- **Era:** the money and health promises, the free-offer pushes, and the "lazy readers" and "Girls!" framing need rewriting for today's rules and audience. Keep the appeal, not the wording.
