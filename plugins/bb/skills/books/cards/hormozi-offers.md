# $100M Offers (free course)
**Alex Hormozi.** Read in full, 136 passages.

## The core idea
People buy on perceived value, so make the offer so specific and low-risk that they stop comparing prices. Value rises with the outcome and belief it will work, and falls with waiting and effort. Late on, the argument turns to wrappers: time and naming make the same offer feel new, so when ads tire, change the creative first, then the words, then the wrapper.

## What this changes
- A generic script opens on the brand. This one opens on the viewer's situation, said better than they could say it.
- A generic hook promises a benefit. This one gives the outcome, the time and the thing they are spared.
- A generic close offers 10% off. This one uses free or a massive discount, or adds extras instead.
- A generic offer beat is one list at one price. This one makes the viewer count separate items.
- A tired ad usually gets a new idea. Here it gets new creative first, with the offer kept.

## The ten best passages

`hormozi-offers-0018` · part 1
- **Lesson:** Persuasion is making people feel understood, not teaching them.
- **Do:** Spend the hook and first beat on their situation, in their words, before any pitch.

`hormozi-offers-0032` · part 2
- **Lesson:** Belief that it will work for them is its own source of value, separate from the outcome.
- **Do:** Check the script covers all four: the outcome, why it will work, how soon, how little effort.

`hormozi-offers-0036` · part 2
- **Lesson:** Speed can beat a free rival, and visible progress makes a slow result feel more valuable.
- **Do:** If the result is slow, show an early sign of progress in the first seconds.

`hormozi-offers-0041` · part 2
- **Lesson:** The promise is a specific result, in a set time, without the biggest pain or fear.
- **Do:** Write the hook as outcome, time, then "without" the dreaded thing. Name the buyer if it fits.

`hormozi-offers-0047` · part 2
- **Lesson:** Each objection flips into a plain "how to" line, and plain beats clever.
- **Do:** Turn the top three objections into three hook options, then test them.

`hormozi-offers-0069` · part 3
- **Lesson:** People count items more easily than they judge intangible value, so one offer split up feels bigger.
- **Do:** Give the core product and each extra its own beat or card, numbered on screen.

`hormozi-offers-0102` · part 5
- **Lesson:** Long-gap proof lets new buyers picture where they will be a year on.
- **Do:** Stack "one year later" customer clips back to back, with the time gap on screen.

`hormozi-offers-0127` · part 6
- **Lesson:** Cutting the time makes the same thing worth far more, and it answers "how long will this take?"
- **Do:** Put the time in the first line and on screen as a contrast. Show a timer if it is real.

`hormozi-offers-0129` · part 6
- **Lesson:** Refresh in order: creative, then body copy, then headline or wrapper. The first step leaves the offer as it is.
- **Do:** When a reel tires, swap the video or picture before rewriting anything.

`hormozi-offers-0131` · part 6
- **Lesson:** Mild discounts do not move people on the fence. Free and massive discounts do.
- **Do:** Replace 10% or 20% off with a free item or a deep cut.

## Three questions for the critic
1. Does the first beat describe the viewer's situation before the product appears? (`0018`)
2. Does the hook give a specific outcome, a time and the pain they are spared? (`0041`)
3. If there is a price incentive, is it free or a massive discount, not a mild one? (`0131`)

## What doesn't carry over
- **The edit:** it says little on feel, rhythm or eye. On-screen ideas here are our translation, so use an editing source for the cut.
- **The full bonus routine:** name, prove, picture and price for each bonus (`0071`, `0073`) won't fit in 15 to 60 seconds. Use one line per extra, or feature one.
- **Bonus value:** bonuses worth more than the core (`0069`) suits a service better than a physical product.
- **Business-building material:** margins, the delivery cube, partner deals and performance pricing (`0051` to `0066`, `0075` to `0077`, `0097` to `0100`) are not script material.
- **The examples:** most come from gyms, coaching and high-ticket services. The "one year later" campaign used videos from clients who had stayed over a year, so reorders and long-term use are our translation.
- **The numbers:** the $112 million, 30 to one and return-on-spend figures are his own claims. Do not use them as proof or benchmarks.
- **The refresh schedule:** weekly creative and new copy every two to three weeks (`0132`) is his rhythm. Let the account's own data set the timing.
- **Urgency:** it only works if true. Do not write a deadline, stock count or guarantee the client cannot honour.
