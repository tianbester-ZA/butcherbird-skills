# Diagnose: what to settle before a hook exists

Contents: the appeal · what they do now · how aware · how many times they've heard it · what only this brand has · where the books disagree · writing it down

Answer these for each ad slot. One line per answer, each with a passage id, in `reasoning.md` §1. Doubt about what they don't believe and why the deal exists goes to `offer-and-proof.md`. If the brief, the log or a search can't answer something, pick the sensible reading, log it and carry on.

Every before and after uses an invented brand. All the book evidence is print, mail order, TV or lab. None of it tested a muted feed, so read it as what moves people, not as how a reel works.

## 0. One real person

Before the appeal, write who this is for, from the client's own evidence, not from a type you imagine: a name for her type, her day, what she uses now, the words she'd say (from a review or comment if there is one), the one belief to shift, what would prove it to her and what the footage can't. Say why her and not the client's other personas, with numbers and dates. If the evidence isn't read yet, stop and read it. The casting can still be wide (see "Write for a moment" below); the thinking can't.

## 1. The appeal

**Hunt for the appeal before writing anything.**
Default: start from the product and write hooks until one sounds good.
Books: two mail-order ads with the same space, paper and care differed 19.5 times in sales on appeal alone. Caples would spend eleven months finding it and one week on the ads (`caples-0009`). Of eleven ads, the five winners (502 to 867 replies) all had one appeal in the headline; the six losers (191 to 263) didn't, though some had it in the body (`caples-0077`). So the appeal has to be in the first line, not somewhere later.
Reel: write three different appeals as one line each, then hooks for the strongest.
Before (Moss Mop): three hooks that all say it cleans well. After: "no mop smell in the hall", "floor dry before the kids are home", "one bottle, a year of floors".

**Score desires, don't pick the nicest.**
Default: the desire the brand likes talking about.
Books: every product touches two to four desires and only one can lead. Score each on urgency, staying power and how many people share it (`schwartz-0018`).
Reel: put the three scores in the reasoning sheet. The winner goes in the first line and everything else serves it.

**Teach nothing.**
Default: "Did you know most treats are full of fillers?"
Books: when advertising has to create the want it becomes education, returning at best one dollar per dollar (`schwartz-0015`). Hopkins's tests put each toothbrush convert at $20 to $25, and that maker dropped the idea entirely (`hopkins-0038`).
Before (Dune Dog): "Most owners don't know what's in their dog's treats." After: first frame, a dog ignoring its owner across a park. On screen: "He comes back for chicken. Only chicken."

**Sell the cure.**
Default: "Protect your skin from future damage."
Books: people pay for cure, not prevention (`sugarman-0229`, `hopkins-0040`). Sugarman sold one pill on what it cures and played down what it prevents (`sugarman-0233`). The one prevention line that works puts the harm on someone the viewer loves, because people won't picture disaster for themselves (`schwartz-0076`).
Before (Salt Road sun cream): "Prevent sun damage." After: a child's red shoulders at the end of a beach day. On screen: "You did the kids. Who did you miss?"

**A small benefit you can picture beats a big one you can't.**
Default: "Transform your mornings."
Books: homeowners who just read cable TV's benefits subscribed at 20%; those asked to imagine themselves using it subscribed at 47% (`heath-0184`). It may be tangibility, not size, that makes people care (`heath-0185`).
Before (Kloof Kettle): "The kettle that changes everything." After: one hand presses the button, feet not yet out of bed, steam already going.

**Let the picture hand them a role; don't claim it.**
Default: "For the woman who knows what she wants."
Books: a product meets a need and also says who the buyer is (`schwartz-0120`). A flattering role in a wordless image is accepted easily, because nothing has to be defended, unlike a performance claim (`schwartz-0124`). Status comes from how others treat us, so show it through a partner's or a rival's eyes (`hormozi-leads-0184`).
Before (Stoep camp chair): "Built for people who love the outdoors." After: three mates in cheap chairs quietly watching the fourth lean back.

**Don't trust a vote, including ours.**
Default: pick the wittiest line.
Books: asked which ad would pull, people chuckled at "Popular Secretary Caught Red Handed" and voted it the winner; in the split run the plain "Hundreds are Changing to Fragrant Frostilla" got 89% more replies (`caples-0221`). Opinion tests favour clever ads and punish appeals that reflect badly on the voter (`caples-0279`).
Reel: an opinion test is what a blind judge, a critic and a room are. Keep a plain or slightly embarrassing hook among those filmed so the numbers can overrule the room.

## 2. What they do now

**The rival is the habit, not the lookalike.**
Default: compare with the category.
Books: a canvass found only 4% used any canned pork and beans and 96% baked at home, so "buy my brand" spoke to 4% and the appeal that worked was against home baking (`hopkins-0043`). Competitors are whatever else answers the same moment: "something to wake me up" is coffee, Red Bull or a walk (`sharp-0178`). Hopkins's test: what would you say to one man using a rival's soap on a train (`hopkins-0047`)?
Before (Bergwind biltong): "The best biltong in Joburg." After: a man at a garage till, holding the R40 packet, looks at it, looks at the lens.

**Write for a moment, not a persona.**
Default: "busy mum, 28 to 35" and a script for each.
Books: Ford and Chevrolet owners came out essentially identical on personality and demographics in 1959, and it was repeated across categories (`sharp-0057`). Brands in a category sell to nearly the same people (`sharp-0174`). What changes is the occasion: healthy today, a treat tomorrow (`sharp-0178`).
Reel: put the buying moment in line one ("Gift for someone who has everything") and cast wide. One flattering label can still earn its place: Swedish shoppers bought ecological bananas 32% of the time; a price sign took that to 46%, and "Hello Environmentalists" took it to 51% (`cialdini-0330`).

**Identity beats similarity, if it's real.**
Default: "people like you love it".
Books: Cialdini's 2021 chapter separates "like us" from "one of us": shared place, family, tribe (`cialdini-0376`).
Reel: said in the first second, in the right accent, only if it's true of the person on camera. None of the unity evidence comes from ads, so test it.

## 3. How aware

The want comes first; this table says what the opening does with it.

| Viewer | Open with | Not with | Passage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Their own situation or attitude, so the right people recognise themselves. It should turn away non-prospects. | Price, product, what it does, or the desire. These come later. | `schwartz-0036`, `schwartz-0049` |
| Problem aware | The need in their words, felt until it costs something. | The product name in line one. | `schwartz-0033` |
| Solution aware | Name the result, prove it can happen, then show the product is how. | Mechanism first. That's for stale markets, not for awareness. | `schwartz-0031` |
| Product aware | What they don't yet know or believe about it. | Restating what they know. | `schwartz-0029` |
| Most aware | The name and the bargain, with a believable reason for it. | Invented urgency. Deadlines and stock counts are our practice, not Schwartz's, and only when true. | `schwartz-0028` |

**Sometimes cold is too cold.** If they know so little that a gap is an abyss, a question won't pull them; one quick frame of place, people or stakes makes them care (`heath-0091`).

**Contradicting a belief** ("you think X, it's actually Y") needs authority strong enough to get away with it, aims only to loosen the old belief, and goes straight to the positive claim (`schwartz-0162`). Without a doctor, a test on screen or a number, lead with a symptom they recognise.

## 4. How many times they've heard it

Sophistication is a separate dial from awareness.

- First in market: say the claim plainly and big. `schwartz-0051`
- Second: copy the working claim and push it further. `schwartz-0053`
- Heard it all: change how it works, not how big it is. `schwartz-0054`, `schwartz-0055`, and the mechanism can become the hook itself: "a new way, even if everything else failed" (`schwartz-0186`).
- Mechanisms worn out: sharpen the mechanism. `schwartz-0059`
- Believes nothing: identification, who the buyer gets to be. `schwartz-0060`

**What the viewer wants is what everyone says.**
Default: lead with the obvious want.
Books: a message built only on what the prospect wants sounds familiar and gets screened out. Reeves didn't sell chocolate taste; he found the shell that melts in your mouth, not in your hand (`bencivenga-0074`).
Before (Tannie Bee honey): "Pure golden honey." After: a jar turned upside down, honey doesn't move. On screen: "Why ours won't pour."

**In a crowded market, go to the extreme and word it oddly.**
Default: a louder category promise.
Books: in a sexual-health market where everyone said the same thing, Schwartz wrote "71-year-old man has sexual congress five times a day", the extreme case in an old-fashioned phrase, and it pulled 9% first time out (`masters-0053`).
Reel: for a tired category, find the oldest customer, the longest wear, the strangest use, and one word nobody in the category uses.

**Tell what nobody has bothered to tell.**
Default: "premium quality".
Books: every brewer shouted "pure". One showed the plate glass cooling room, bottles washed four times and 1,018 yeast experiments. Any brewer could have said it; he was first, and made the greatest success in beer advertising (`hopkins-0029`).
Before (Fynn hiking socks): "Premium merino socks." After: "Forty-one pairs walked to holes before we changed the heel." Forty-one of something, on a table.

## 5. What only this brand has

**Most ads never get linked to the brand.**
Default: logo at the end, because it's an ad and they'll know.
Books: of 143 Australian TV ads, about 40% were recognised and about 40% of those linked to the right brand, so roughly 16% of exposures cleared both hurdles (`sharp-0011`). Marketers massively overestimate their own assets; what counts is fame and uniqueness, and only strangers can tell you (`sharp-0117`).
Reel: assets are how a viewer links the ad to the brand (`sharp-0195`). Our rule: pick two (pack, colour, sound, a recurring prop or face) and put them in the first seconds of every ad. Then ask someone who doesn't know the brand whose ad it is.

**A great idea can build the idea, not the brand.**
Default: a funny concept with the product bolted on.
Books: Miller Lite's "man law" films got plenty of attention and were shared, but viewers remembered them as the man law ads, not Miller Lite, and share kept falling until they were dropped (`sharp-0193`).
Reel: if a viewer can retell the joke without naming the product, put the product inside the joke before filming.

List the client's own ideas here too. Each is used, or dropped with a reason in the sheet.

## Where the books disagree

- **Brand early or not.** Ogilvy says brand within ten seconds and Sharp wants distinctive assets in every ad (`ogilvy-0105`, `sharp-0195`); Schwartz says a cold viewer gets no name, no product and no price (`schwartz-0036`). We lean: the pack, colour and look are in frame from the first second; the words say nothing about them.
- **Wide or narrow.** Sharp says all category buyers (`sharp-0057`); Cialdini's label and Schwartz's identification want a named viewer (`cialdini-0330`, `schwartz-0049`). We lean: wide casting and a first line that names a moment, not a type.
- **Problem or result.** Hopkins says, from his own experience and with no test shown, that positive ads outpull negative ones four to one (`hopkins-0068`); Miller wants negative stakes because people fear loss more (`miller-0089`). We lean: one quick, light beat of the problem, then the after-state.

## Writing it down

```
### Slot 2: second bag free
appeal: he comes back when called; urgency 3, staying 3, scope 2 (schwartz-0018)
what they do now: chicken from the fridge in a bag (hopkins-0043)
awareness: unaware of the brand, knows treats exist; open on the scene (schwartz-0036)
heard it: "dogs love it" is worn out, so show how (schwartz-0055)
belief gap: "my dog's fussy" forms at the first bite; show the fussy dog eat (bencivenga-0005)
offer: second bag free, because a batch was over-baked this week (schwartz-0187)
format: owner and dog, park, handheld, camera is the dog, 15 s, about 35 words
only this brand: the founder's three-legged dog; red bag in every shot (sharp-0195)
client's ideas: recall at the park challenge (used)
readings taken: none
```
